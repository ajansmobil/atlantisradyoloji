DESIGN AND CONSTRUCTION OF X-RAY AND MAMMOGRAPHY ROOMS

X-ray and mammography rooms require less lead shielding than CT rooms as they use low-energy X-rays, but radiation safety remains critical. With 23 years of experience, we provide expert services for X-ray and mammography room design, lead shielding, and turnkey construction. X-ray systems typically generate 50-125 kV, while mammography units produce 25-35 kV low-energy X-rays.

In X-ray room planning, lead equivalent is calculated based on the device's kV value. Standard X-ray rooms generally require 1.5-2 mm lead equivalent. Wall and ceiling shielding use lead sheets or leaded plaster. Leaded glass windows (2-3 mm Pb eq) are mandatory for control consoles. Mammography rooms require only 1-1.5 mm lead equivalent due to lower energy, but special waiting areas and changing rooms are important for patient comfort and privacy.

X-ray rooms are typically 15-20 m², while mammography rooms require 12-15 m². Ceiling heights are maintained at 2.5-3 m. Electrical infrastructure requires 220V/1-phase power supply. Ventilation must provide 4-6 air changes per hour. X-ray systems generate low heat, so cooling is generally unnecessary, though mammography units may require compressor cooling. Special patient tables and column mounting are installed for patient positioning.

In our turnkey X-ray and mammography room projects, we manage TAEK licensing and radiation safety reporting processes. Post-installation radiation measurement tests (leakage tests) are conducted. For mammography rooms, we offer special design solutions emphasizing female patient privacy. We are experienced in adapting existing X-ray rooms for digital systems and modernizing for CR (Computed Radiography) and DR (Digital Radiography) systems. All projects comply with ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principles and ICRP standards.
